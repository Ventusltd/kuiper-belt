See Through: fall from a field of stars to any of 5,800 UK substations, then watch it rise in 3D on the real satellite ground, drawn by rule from open map data, with its pylons.
Type a substation name and press GO. Drag to orbit, pinch or wheel to zoom.
The map, imagery and terrain need the internet. Credits are in NOTICE.md.

Home: the Kuiper editions (../../editions/). Once a substation is chosen, the row STAR KEY <k> · OPEN IT ON THE KUIPER opens
the Kuiper at that star's key (../../editions/#key=<k>); the X among the stars goes back to the Kuiper.

Links into this page are its hash (the stable link see-through/ forwards it):
  #sub=Drax                          types the name and presses GO (the exact name, else the first name that contains it)
  #key=36064705400                   one amber ring where the Kuiper law puts that key; the star that lands on a substation
                                     fills the box ("THE STAR THAT LANDS ON <NAME>. PRESS GO")
  #key=<k>&space=<S>                 a star key cut under another sky S is remapped to this sky ("STAR KEY REMAPPED · SKY …")
  #repo=gridatlas                    lights the stars whose keys fall in that repository's ranges of cosmos/wafer.tsv
  #sub=Cowley&view=3d                the view (kuiper, map, sat, 3d) after the flight
key with space is the identity; sub is a search that lands on the first named match. On arrival the page writes
#key=<k>&space=<S>&sub=<name>, and that link opens the same substation again.
The sky is the Kuiper's address space, read from ../../cosmos/wafer-meta.json; kuiper-law.js holds the frozen value
(63,340,120,796) used when that file cannot be read.
